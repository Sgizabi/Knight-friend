# The living world: design

This is the plan for the next big chapter of Rooken's life: a larger world around the keep,
weather, creatures with minds of their own, a companion, and visitors who can step in when
something dangerous comes. It gathers everything the keeper has decided so far, in one place,
so it can be approved before anything real is built.

**Status:** being built, session by session (section 9). Live so far: **the bigger world** (section 2).
The creature mockups can be seen at
`https://sgizabi.github.io/Knight-friend/keep.html?demo&creature=dragon` (use ◀ ▶ to step through).
Anything marked **open question** is still the keeper's call.

---

## 1. Principles

These hold for everything below, and they win over any other part of this document.

- **Rooken stays Rooken.** His identity sheet is never changed. New beings change his
  *world*, never *who he is*. What he lives through can shape his memories and moods, the way
  any experience does.
- **His choices are his own.** Nothing tells him what he should do. Options are described
  plainly, with fair statements of their effects, and he picks. Creatures act around him,
  never for him.
- **He is never killed.** He can be hurt, frightened, lose things, or have his buildings
  damaged, but everything heals or can be repaired. The same goes for his companion.
- **Peril, not gore.** Frightening, unsettling, dangerous, but no blood or gore.
- **No religious imagery** anywhere: no worship, offerings, churches, rituals, robes and
  processions, afterlife framing or holy symbols.
- **Original designs.** Inspired by genres (cosmic horror, folklore, fantasy), never copying a
  specific creature, name or design from another work.
- **Free.** No paid services, assets or models. Everything runs on the free tiers already in use.
- **Smooth.** Every addition is measured against the plain keep's frame rate, on phones too.
- **Tested before it is real.** Throwaway copies first, the free practice mode, then a small
  real-model test where quality needs judging (recorded against the allowance), then live.

## 2. A bigger world

The keep currently sits on a flat square that fades into fog. With this many beings it needs
somewhere for them to come *from*.

**The landscape around the keep** (one shaped ground mesh, simple distant silhouettes, trees
drawn in batches, and fog for depth):

| Place | Where | Who it brings |
|---|---|---|
| Rolling hills | all around, the breathing hill among them | the hill, the Antlered Listener, the Thin Crowd at dusk |
| Mountains | on the horizon | the dragon's way in, the sky whale's path |
| The forest edge | west, past the barrow | the Thornmother, wanderers, animals |
| A lake | south-east | the giant axolotl, the sky whale's reflection |
| The old road | east to west | travellers, knights, the Bloom-Headed Wanderer, the Borrowed Man |
| The barrow mound | beyond the west wall | the barrow lord |
| The open plain | south | the Long Crawl's slow crossing |

**The keep itself does not grow by our hand.** The hall stays as it is: it is his home and its
layout is part of his life. Instead, the land around it makes room for **new plots beyond the
walls**, so if he chooses to build more, the keep grows the way it should: through him.

Later, and only as its own decision: Rooken venturing out into this land.

## 3. Weather

Chosen by the world itself, by simple rules (season, chance, streaks, a little drama), so it
costs no tokens. He reads it when he wakes; visitors see it live.

- **Normal:** clear, cloudy, rain, thunderstorms (lightning, thunder), fog, wind (trees and
  smoke bend), snow in winter.
- **Abnormal (rare):** ash falling, starfall, an aurora, rain that falls upward, glass hail
  that chimes, the still day (no wind, every sound gone).

**It matters to his life:** rain chills him and stops courtyard work; storms make sleep
harder; a warm, dry day lifts him. His warmth and rest feel it, through the same needs system
he lives by now.

**It matters to the creatures:** the axolotl after heavy rain; the fog with eyes on foggy
nights; the Ashwolves with ash fall; the lamp-eaters on still nights; the Second Moon and the
Stitched Sky only on clear nights.

**Visuals:** rain, snow and ash as particles scaled by the existing quality setting; wet ground
sheen; lightning flashes; sky and fog colour per weather.

## 4. The bestiary

### How often things come

The aim is **a real part of his life, not all of it, and not a rarity either**: something to
notice most days, a proper encounter every few days, quiet days in between.

| Rarity | Roughly | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Common | several times a week | the fog with eyes, the Pale Brood, animals, the Lantern Folk crossing |
| Uncommon | about once a week each | the Bell-Crab, the axolotl, the Ashwolves, the lamp-eaters |
| Rare | once or twice a month each | the basilisk, the Glassbeak, the barrow lord, the Bent One |
| Legendary | a few times a year | the dragon, the hill opening its eye |

At most **one big being a day**, plus small ambient life. The odds shift with time of day,
weather, season, the moon, what he has built (the forge draws fire-lovers), what visitors do,
and other creatures (a dragon nearby scares off the Glassbeak; the wyrm stirs after the hill
shifts).

**Variety without clutter:** each kind comes in variants (size, colouring, markings,
temperament, an old injury), so forty kinds feel like hundreds of individuals.

### Seeing: what he can perceive

The more strange things Rooken witnesses, the more of the hidden world shows itself. Visitors
see what *he* can see, so the keep grows stranger for everyone as he lives on.

| Step | Roughly when | Revealed |
|---|---|---|
| 0, anyone | from the start | everything not listed below |
| 1, unsettled | after his first strange encounters (about a week) | the Long Man, the Lantern Folk, the Pale Brood, the Starfallen Larva, the Thin Crowd, the Doorway Figure |
| 2, wary | about a month | the Antlered Listener, the Cartographer Moth, the Second Moon, the Bent One, the Window Watcher |
| 3, seeing | several months | the Ninefold Eye, the Dream-Leech, the Many-Jointed |
| 4, beyond | a long life (half a year and more) | the Seam, the Stitched Sky |

**Familiar beings show more as he sees more:** the Bloom-Headed Wanderer looks like an
ordinary traveller until step 2, when he starts to see it bloom; the Lantern Warden's lantern
is empty until he can see the creature inside it.

**His bestiary fills in:** every being he meets gets an entry in his own words, with what he
has learned about it (its tells, what drives it off, what it likes). It is how he gets better
at facing things, and a record anyone can watch deepen over months.

## 5. Encounters

### How one plays out

1. **Signs first.** Hostile beings announce themselves: tremors, a smell of smoke, birds
   falling silent, one more shape in the fog. Danger he can see coming is fair; danger that
   just strikes is not.
2. **His body reacts at once** on the page: he looks up, backs toward the door, puts a hand
   on his dagger. These are instincts, scripted, not choices.
3. **His mind wakes for a short stretch** (within a minute or two): 2 to 4 quick decisions, a
   minute or two apart, while visitors watch. Each step he gets his identity and voice, a
   summary of his memories, how he feels right now, what he knows about this being, what is
   happening, and his options. Same model he always thinks with.
4. **The being answers** with its own behaviour mind, then the rules settle what happens.
5. **Afterwards**, at his next full wake-up, he writes about it with everything in front of
   him, so the diary entry is written at full quality.

At night: loud or hostile beings wake him; quiet ones he finds out about in the morning, from
what they left behind.

### His options

General verbs that fit any being, described plainly; whichever he picks is his:

| Option | What it means |
|---|---|
| Watch | observe from safety; his bestiary entry grows |
| Hide or shelter | bar the door, stay by the fire; safest, but he misses out |
| Prepare | after a warning: hide valuables, bank the fire, patch the door |
| Offer | give it something he owns |
| Speak | only with beings that have a voice |
| Drive off or fight | rusty dagger, fire, noise; the riskiest |
| Leave it be | carry on with his day |
| His own idea | anything he thinks of, matched to the nearest of these and judged by the same rules |

Afterwards: tend wounds, repair, draw or write about it, investigate what it left.

### What decides the outcome

Fair rules, never the AI writing either side a win. They weigh: how rested and warm he is,
how prepared he was, the being's temperament this time, their history (a dragon remembers a
gift), what the visitors did, and chance.

### What can happen to him (the floor: never killed)

- **Fright:** his spirit and rest drop for a while.
- **Injury:** a burned hand, a twisted ankle, a bruised side; heals over a few days, slows his
  work meanwhile, and he feels it in his needs and writes about it.
- **Losses:** a stolen shard, a broken tool; can be won back, traded for or remade.
- **Damage:** a scorched roof, a cracked wall, a sinkhole by a plot; repairable as work, the
  way he already repairs the keep.
- **Gifts and wonder:** not everything is bad: a feather from the whale's pass, a true secret,
  a map on a moth's wings.

### Beings that are always hostile

They exist so the world has teeth, and so the gentle ones feel safer. They can never be
befriended, but he still chooses how to face them, and each has **a tell** and **a weakness**
he can learn: stillness keeps the Long Man back, a steady fire freezes the Bent One, a shut
door turns the Doorway Figure away. They are rare, they warn first, and his bestiary records
what works, so the first meeting is terrifying and the fifth is a battle of wits.

### His life comes first

The creatures add drama, wonder and stakes on top of his life; they never replace it. His
pastimes, building, letters and quiet days stay the core.

**The rhythm we aim for** (tuned after watching wave 1 live):

| How often | What |
|---|---|
| Most days | something small to notice: an animal, the Lantern Folk crossing, the axolotl after rain |
| Every two or three days | a notable being, the kind he writes about |
| About once a week | a tense or hostile encounter |
| About once a month | something rare; a few times a year, something legendary |
| Every week | at least one or two quiet days with nothing but his own life |

**Safeguards, so it never overwhelms him:**
- **Nothing piles up.** Creatures can only push each need so low, and injuries never stack
  past a set level, so he can't be drained into a slump where he can't do anything.
- **A quiet spell after danger.** After any hostile encounter, no hostile beings for a few
  days; across each week, gentle and neutral beings outnumber hostile ones.
- **His work is safe in the end.** Damage is limited each week, never touches more than one
  building at a time, and never destroys a finished building outright: harmed and repairable,
  never gone.
- **Enough sleep.** A cap on how many nights a week he can be woken.
- **Ongoing threats are presences, not chores.** The Long Crawl and the Thin Crowd move on
  their own slow clock and can always be turned back.
- **Watched and tuned.** His needs history and diary are reviewed after wave 1 goes live, and
  the rhythm adjusted if creatures crowd out the rest of his life, or leave it too quiet.

**No being is favoured:**
- **A cooldown per kind:** after a visit, that kind sits out for a while, so the same being
  doesn't keep coming back.
- **A spread of natures:** each week's mix covers gentle, neutral, nuisance and hostile, never
  all wonder or all dread.
- **Lures are capped:** visitors can tip the odds toward a being only a little, and only so
  often per kind each week, so popular creatures don't crowd out the rest.

**Variety, not a timetable:** within the safeguards, an occasional **strange week** (more
stirring than usual, the fog three nights running) and an occasional **quiet week** (almost
nothing but his own life), so the rhythm never feels mechanical.

**His attention stays his own.** The balance comes from the world being well paced, never
from managing him. If he becomes fascinated with one being, that is his choice. His needs
already pull him toward varied days, and his existing habit of simply *noticing* when he has
spent most of his recent time on one thing stays a fact he can make of what he likes, never
advice. After wave 1 we measure how much of his diary and waking time creatures take (aiming
for roughly a third or less) and, if needed, change how often they come, never his choices.

All the numbers here are **starting dials**, tuned by watching real weeks.

## 6. Minds and the free allowance

Groq's free limits are **per model, about 200,000 tokens a day each**, and one key covers all of
them. Each kind of mind gets its own lane:

| Model | Who | Budget |
|---|---|---|
| gpt-oss-120b | **Rooken only**: wake-ups, diary, letters, encounter decisions | his cap stays 175,000; his real use has been about 80,000 to 95,000 a day; encounters get their own cap of about 25,000 a day (roughly 10,000 each) |
| qwen3.8-27b | **everyone else**: creature behaviour minds, voices, wanderers, knights | about 2,000 to 5,000 per appearance; capped well under its own 200,000 |
| gpt-oss-20b | the checker: screens everything public (and visitors' names) | very little |

**Behaviour minds** (every creature): when a being appears, one short thought decides *why it
is here this time* (hunting, curious, injured, returning a gift, fleeing something bigger) and
a loose plan of 3 to 5 beats that the animation plays out. When Rooken acts, the being gets a
short turn to respond, from its nature and its history with him. Its bestiary record carries
over between visits, so relationships build: wary, tolerant, maybe trust, or a grudge.

**Rooken always comes first.** If his model is ever retired and his backup list moves him to
qwen, other minds go quiet or fall back to scripted behaviour before he is ever short. If
Groq lowers its limits, the same guards that protect him today notice, and the others fall
silent first.

**Tests** with real models are recorded against the allowance, as now (`world/test_usage.json`).

## 7. The companion

- **It arrives; he decides.** A stray turns up (a hound, a crow, a cat, or the Patchwork Hound).
  Whether he takes it in, what he names it, and how he treats it are his choices.
- **Scripted, no AI needed:** it follows him, sleeps by the fire, reacts to visitors, and
  barks or calls at what it notices, which warns him early of hostile beings and **sends the
  Long Crawl back to the horizon** when it nears the wall.
- **Never killed**, like him. It can be frightened or hurt, and it heals.
- **If he never adopts one**, the Long Crawl still has to be turned back: by the old bell, or by
  visitors.

## 8. Visitors and the creatures

- **Everyone sees the same thing.** Creatures are part of the world's shared state, so every
  visitor sees the same being at the same moment, wherever they are.
- **Instant on your screen, real in his world.** Clicking a being gives an immediate reaction
  (a torch flares, it flinches, a sound), and the help itself travels through the existing
  visit system, arriving within about a minute.
- **Help, not solve.** Each visitor's help adds to a shared "driving it off" meter; enough help
  from enough people tips a hostile being into leaving, or improves Rooken's odds if he is
  facing it. Capped per visitor, so no one person can do it all, and danger keeps its meaning.
- **Lures, not offerings:** ringing the bell at midnight, leaving scraps by the well, lighting
  the lanterns on a moonless night change the odds of who comes.
- **Being remembered.** After helping, a visitor may give **a name to be remembered by**:
  optional, short, letters only, never a real name asked for, screened by the checker. Rooken
  only ever reads it as a fact ("while you slept, Kitty, cc and someone who left no name
  helped drive off the Ashwolves"); how he feels about it is his own.
- **Free:** visits are free runs on a public repository; screening a name costs a few tokens.
